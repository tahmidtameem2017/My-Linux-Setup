YOUR DESKTOP, ONE SECTION AT A TIME

Everything here is a shortcut. Open a heading, read one line, close it. Nothing else is going on. Press / to search instead of scrolling, or Mod+Alt+H to bring this window back later.

/ /
What the words mean
Mod
The Windows / Super key. Every shortcut here starts with it.
Column
One window. Windows sit side by side instead of on top of each other.
Workspace
A separate screenful of windows — tabs for your whole desktop.
The bar
The thin strip at the top: clock, sound, wifi, battery, power.
The island
The middle of the bar: weather and clock, plus anything you switch on — like the performance monitor, Mod+Alt+P.
Popup
A small panel over the desktop. Esc closes it.
Screenshot menu
The one panel holding every capture tool — region, window, screen, scrolling, recording and text extraction. Mod+Shift+5.
Dictation
You talk, the words are typed into whatever you were already typing in. Entirely on this machine — no account, no internet. Mod+Alt+D.
Preview
Look at a file without opening it. Inside the launcher, Ctrl+Space.
Now Playing
The music card under the bar — what is playing, plus a player for your own ~/Music. Mod+Alt+M.

Nothing matches that. Try one word — wallpaper, screenshot, wifi.

How the screenshot menu itself works

The menu is the one place every capture tool lives, and it is entirely keyboard-driven. Pick a row with ↑ ↓ or j k, press 1–9 to jump straight to one, then Enter to run it. Esc closes it without doing anything. The digit only moves the selection — it never starts a capture, so a slip of the finger cannot begin a screen recording. The line at the bottom always explains what the selected row will do.

How voice typing behaves

It runs entirely on this machine: nothing is uploaded and there is no account. It listens when you ask and stops on a pause, and it types into whichever window you were already using — a terminal, a chat, a document — rather than opening one of its own.

It is a toggle, not hold-to-talkpress once to start, once to stop
Stop when you go quieta pause ends the recording
Know when it is listeningan overlay, plus a dot in the bar
If the dot in the bar lights up for another program, that one is recording toothe dot follows the microphone, not the app

The Mod+Alt+Escape key is the escape hatch. A normal stop is enough almost every time; it exists for the rare moment where the microphone stays open no matter what you press, and it is safe to mash — it touches nothing else on your computer.

How saving music works

Leave the link box empty and the card saves what is playing right now. Paste a link into it and the card saves that instead — if the link is unusable it tells you why rather than quietly saving something else.

Why a file sometimes is not found

Dot-folders and folders ignored by git are never searched — that is why ~/.config and node_modules stay invisible. The list of files is cached; delete ~/.cache/niri-setup/file-index/ to rebuild it after moving a lot of files around.

The nine wallpaper settings

The menu is for pictures. Deeper settings — how often it rotates, how big the copy may be, jpeg or png, whether the terminal colours follow the picture — live in one small file and are edited with a script. There is no clickable editor for them.

See every setting and its valuescripts/wallpaper-process.sh get
Change how often it rotates… set INTERVAL 10m
Change several at once, checked before writing… setmany DOWNSCALE 1920 FORMAT jpeg
Put everything back as it shipped… reset
